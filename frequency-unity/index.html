Beyond the Capital অপূর্ব (Apurba) × ACND

Two independent producers from Cumilla are proving that Bangladesh’s next underground sound doesn’t need the capital’s permission, and their collaborative single “Initiate” is the evidence.

Electronic Music Culture Report · Cumilla, Bangladesh · 27 October 2026

01

The rave moved out of the capital

Bangladesh’s electronic music scene is changing shape, quietly but for good. For years it lived almost entirely inside Dhaka’s commercial venues, tuned to peak-time EDM. Now a real underground is growing: warehouse spaces, nature retreats, long and immersive soundscapes.

And it is no longer centred on one city. Regional hubs are producing artists who treat the dancefloor less as a party and more as a shared, almost meditative ritual. In Cumilla, two of them are leading the way: অপূর্ব (Apurba) and ACND.

02

The catalyst: “Initiate”

Their partnership now has a record to show for it. “Initiate” is melodic house and techno at full club length: patient, deliberate and built for listeners who stay for the whole journey rather than a quick adrenaline hit. It lives in the space where dark, brooding atmosphere resolves into something uplifting, even spiritual.

An atmospheric opening gives way to layered, tension-building sequences. Then the breakdown strips the percussion away entirely, before everything returns anchored to a relentless, bass-driven groove. It is the kind of moment a whole room waits for.

03

The architects

অপূর্ব (Apurba)

The independent electronic alias of Khalid Bin Alam: a self-taught producer, audio engineer, DJ and A&R, active since 2016. He first drew attention on YouTube as Euphonic (2018–2023), with future house, synthwave and widely shared remixes.

Today his sound is rooted firmly in the underground. His catalogue runs to 46 works, including the 2026 psychedelic bass house single “1by1” and the Billboard Bangladesh–featured “Nitol Paye Electric” (2025). As A&R for Badenstock Records and curator of the PlayLSD series, he shapes the scene’s structure as much as its sound.

ACND

An emerging Cumilla-based producer who has carved out his own lane in deep, groove-driven melodic house and techno. ACND’s records are hypnotic and spiritual at their core.

That is exactly why the pairing works: where অপূর্ব expands, with cinematic experiments and organic field-recording textures, ACND grounds. One is the counterweight to the other.

04

The partnership, and the B2B ritual

“Initiate” didn’t come from nowhere. Both artists are from Cumilla and have built their careers side by side, sharing production, sound design and even marketing. That studio chemistry is now moving to the stage.

As Bangladesh’s rave culture matures, the back-to-back (B2B) set has become essential: two artists, one set of decks, a live and unscripted conversation. The upcoming অপূর্ব × ACND B2B is one of the most anticipated moments in that story, a masterclass in selection and pacing from two producers who already finish each other’s records.

They aren’t just performing. They are holding space for a growing community, and proving that artists from outside the capital can build a world-class sound and take the regional underground to an international standard.

05

Quick answers

Who are অপূর্ব (Apurba) and ACND?

Two independent electronic producers and DJs from Cumilla, Bangladesh. অপূর্ব is the alias of Khalid Bin Alam, active since 2016; ACND is an emerging producer known for deep, groove-driven melodic house and techno.

What is “Initiate”?

Their collaborative single: club-length melodic techno at 120 BPM that moves from dark atmosphere to uplifting resolution, with a percussion-free breakdown before a bass-driven groove returns.

What is a B2B DJ set?

Back-to-back: two DJs share one set of decks and trade tracks live, with no script. অপূর্ব and ACND’s B2B brings their studio partnership to the stage.

Where can I hear them together?

Their Qbic Pulse set for Hong Kong Community Radio plays on the I Rave You homepage, and “Initiate” is on SoundCloud.
